## 4) What Went Well

- The team delivered the full deterministic training-core slice end to end, not isolated helpers.
- Architecture rules held under pressure: science-driven configuration, deterministic fallback, ML isolation, typed boundaries, and extensive regression coverage.
- Review feedback was consistently converted into concrete fixes plus new regression tests rather than one-off patches.
- Testing discipline was a major strength. Story artifacts show progression from targeted unit tests to integration coverage, and the current repository state validates at `336 passed`.
- Safety-oriented behavior improved materially during the epic: no-history safeguards, deterministic ordering, stronger JSON handling, recovery scaling, and PUOS enforcement all became more robust through the implementation cycle.

## 5) Challenges and Friction

- Contract ambiguity surfaced repeatedly during implementation instead of being eliminated before coding.
- Several stories needed multiple review-fix passes to resolve correctness gaps around rounding semantics, fallback behavior, ordering, fatigue estimation, and malformed input handling.
- Status governance improved but was not fully solved. The sprint tracker still contains an Epic 4 story-key typo, and Epic 5 was already marked `in-progress` before this retrospective was completed.
- Cross-story coupling was higher than expected. Adaptation, readiness, recap/summary, and planning behavior depended on subtle shared assumptions that were only fully aligned late in the epic.

## 6) Deep Story Patterns

### Common Struggles

- Specification mismatches: cable rounding, machine rounding, fatigue lookback semantics, and no-history adaptation behavior all required clarification after implementation had already started.
- Deterministic ordering edge cases: same-day/tied-session selection and date precision required several follow-up fixes.
- Data-hardening issues: malformed JSON handling, stale readiness rows, tracker-key drift, and canonical-name lookup all emerged as recurring quality themes.
- Operational assumptions were sometimes implicit rather than explicit, especially where Epic 4 prepared the path for Epic 5 ML workflows.

### Breakthrough Moments

- The team repeatedly turned review findings into stronger platform contracts instead of local fixes.
- The move from placeholder ML-feature behavior to real feature-vector construction materially improved Epic 4’s usefulness as the deterministic base for Epic 5.
- `WorkoutPlanner` integration into `workout_start` closed an important architecture loop and made planner output operational rather than theoretical.

### Team Collaboration Highlights

- QA and review loops materially improved delivery quality by forcing deterministic coverage on edge cases.
- Architecture guidance remained visible through implementation, especially on config-driven thresholds, absolute imports, no global science config, and ML isolation.
- The team showed good resilience in finishing complex stories without compromising the deterministic-core principle.

## 10) Significant Discovery Check

Significant discoveries affecting next-epic planning: **Yes**

### Significant Changes Identified

1. **Contract ambiguity remained a major source of rework even in a deterministic epic**
   - Impact: Epic 5 will amplify this risk because queue, worker, and model contracts span more components and more failure modes.

2. **The quality of fallback behavior depends on real historical context, not placeholder assumptions**
   - Impact: Epic 5 planning must treat feature construction, deterministic selection, and no-history safeguards as first-class requirements, not implementation details.

3. **Process governance is still behind technical maturity**
   - Impact: asynchronous ML work will be harder to control if tracker validation and closure discipline are not improved first.

### Epic Update Recommendation

Epic 5 planning should be reviewed before the worker/model stories advance. The direction is still sound, but execution assumptions should be tightened around contracts, integration testing, and operability.
