## Section 1: APRE — Auto-Regulatory Progressive Resistance Exercise

### Background & Source

**Primary source:** Knight, K.L. (1979). "Knee rehabilitation by the daily adjustable progressive resistive exercise technique." *American Journal of Sports Medicine*, 7(6), 336–337.

**Validation:** Mann, J.B. et al. (2010). "The Effect of Autoregulatory Progressive Resistance Exercise vs. Linear Periodization on Strength Improvement in College Athletes." *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 24(7), 1718–1723. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20543732/)

**Recent systematic review:** Balsalobre-Fernández, C. et al. (2025). "Autoregulated resistance training for maximal strength enhancement: A systematic review and network meta-analysis." — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12336695/)

### Protocol Structure

All APRE variants follow a 4-set structure:

| Set | Load | Goal |
|-----|------|------|
| 1 | 50% of target RM | Warm-up |
| 2 | 75% of target RM | Activation |
| 3 | 100% of target RM | AMRAP (determines Set 4 weight) |
| 4 | Adjusted weight (see table) | AMRAP (determines next session weight) |

### APRE-6 Adjustment Table (Set 3 → Set 4)

_Source: Knight (1979), reproduced in Mann et al. (2010)_

| Reps on Set 3 | Weight Adjustment for Set 4 |
|---------------|------------------------------|
| ≤ 4 reps | −5 to −10 lbs (−2.5 to −5 kg) |
| 5–7 reps | Same weight |
| 8–10 reps | +5 to +10 lbs (+2.5 to +5 kg) |
| ≥ 11 reps | +10 to +15 lbs (+5 to +7.5 kg) |

### APRE-6 Next-Session Weight Adjustment (based on Set 4 reps)

| Reps on Set 4 | Next Session Adjustment |
|---------------|--------------------------|
| ≤ 4 reps | −5 to −10 lbs |
| 5–7 reps | Same weight (this becomes new 6RM) |
| 8–10 reps | +5 to +10 lbs |
| ≥ 11 reps | +10 to +15 lbs |

### APRE-10 Adjustment Table

_Source: Mann, J.B. et al. (2010). "The Effect of Autoregulatory Progressive Resistance Exercise vs. Linear Periodization on Strength Improvement in College Athletes." JSCR, 24(7), 1718–1723. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20543732/)_

| Reps on Set 3 | Weight Adjustment for Set 4 |
|---------------|------------------------------|
| ≤ 6 reps | −5 to −10 lbs |
| 7–10 reps | Same weight |
| 11–14 reps | +5 to +10 lbs |
| ≥ 15 reps | +10 to +15 lbs |

### Metric Conversion Note (lbs → kg)

APRE adjustment tables are originally specified in **pounds** (Knight, 1979). Metric conversion applies the standard barbell plate increment: **5 lbs ≈ 2.5 kg** (exact: 5 lbs = 2.268 kg; rounded up to the nearest standard 2.5 kg plate). This rounding is universally accepted in international strength training practice — 2.5 kg is the smallest standard barbell plate available in metric gyms. All kg values in these tables reflect this conversion.

| Imperial | Metric (rounded) |
|----------|-----------------|
| 5 lbs | 2.5 kg |
| 10 lbs | 5 kg |
| 15 lbs | 7.5 kg |

### Effectiveness

APRE demonstrated greater 1RM bench press and estimated 1RM squat improvements vs. linear periodization over 6 weeks (Mann et al., 2010). HRV-referenced review (PMC7706636) confirms autoregulation methods match or exceed fixed periodization for strength outcomes.

**Confidence: HIGH** — Multiple peer-reviewed replications; original source clearly documented.

## Section 2: Double Progression

### Definition

Double Progression = progressive overload on two variables simultaneously: **reps** (within a target range) then **weight** (when top of range is achieved for all sets).

### Protocol Rules

1. Select a rep range for each exercise (e.g., 6–8 reps)
2. Train at a given weight until all prescribed sets hit the **top** of the range
3. When all sets hit top of range → increase weight by smallest available increment
4. Repeat from bottom of range with new weight

### Recommended Rep Ranges by Goal

_Source: Schoenfeld, B.J. & Grgic, J. (2021). "Loading Recommendations for Muscle Strength, Hypertrophy, and Local Endurance: A Re-Examination of the Repetition Continuum." *Sports*, 9(2), 32._ — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)

| Goal | Rep Range | % 1RM |
|------|-----------|-------|
| Strength (primary) | 1–5 reps | 80–100% |
| Hypertrophy (primary) | 6–12 reps | 60–80% |
| Muscular Endurance | 15–30 reps | <60% |
| Hypertrophy (wide range) | 6–30 reps | Any load to near-failure |

**Key finding (Schoenfeld 2021):** Hypertrophy can be achieved across a wide rep spectrum (6–30+) when sets are taken to near-failure and volume is equated. Strength gains (1RM) favour heavier loads (>80% 1RM).

### Weight Increment Recommendations

| Exercise Type | Weight Increment |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Compound (barbell) | 2.5 kg (5 lbs) |
| Isolation / small muscle | 1.25 kg (2.5 lbs) |
| Exercises with large % jumps (e.g., lateral raise) | Use wider rep range (10–20) |

**Confidence: HIGH** — Protocol is well-established; rep range data from peer-reviewed meta-analysis.

## Section 3: PUOS & Volume Landmarks (Israetel MEV/MAV/MRV)

### Primary Source

Israetel, M., Hoffmann, J., & Case, J. (2019). *Scientific Principles of Strength Training.* Renaissance Periodization. Volume landmarks data also published at [RP Strength](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth).

> ⚠️ **Attribution note:** "Muscle and Strength Pyramids" is a separate work by **Eric Helms**, Andrea Valdez, and Andy Morgan (2015) — it is NOT an Israetel publication. These are two distinct works by different authors in the S&C space. Israetel's primary texts are "Scientific Principles of Strength Training" (2019) and the RP Strength online content.

**⚠️ Note:** Volume landmarks are NOT from a single peer-reviewed RCT. They represent Israetel's synthesis of available literature + practical coaching data. Treat as evidence-informed guidelines with HIGH practical validity, not fixed absolute numbers.

### Volume Landmark Definitions

| Landmark | Definition |
|----------|-----------|
| **MV** (Maintenance Volume) | Minimum sets to maintain current muscle mass |
| **MEV** (Minimum Effective Volume) | Minimum sets to stimulate muscle growth |
| **MAV** (Maximum Adaptive Volume) | Optimal zone for maximum hypertrophic adaptation |
| **MRV** (Maximum Recoverable Volume) | Upper limit; exceeding prevents adequate recovery |

### Volume Landmarks by Muscle Group (sets per week)

_Source: Israetel (RP Strength, Renaissance Periodization series)_

| Muscle Group | MV | MEV | MAV | MRV |
|-------------|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| Back | 8 | 10 | 14–22 | 25+ |
| Chest | 8 | 10 | 12–20 | 22+ |
| Quads | 6 | 8 | 12–18 | 20+ |
| Hamstrings | 4 | 6 | 10–16 | 20+ |
| Glutes | 0 | 0 | 4–12 | 16+ |
| Shoulders (side/rear) | 0 | 8 | 16–22 | 26+ |
| Shoulders (front) | 0 | 0 | 6–8 | 12+ |
| Biceps | 5 | 8 | 14–20 | 26+ |
| Triceps | 4 | 6 | 10–14 | 18+ |
| Calves | 6 | 8 | 12–16 | 20+ |
| Abs/Core | 0 | 8 | 16–20 | 25+ |
| Lower Back | 4 | 6 | 10–14 | 16+ |

### PUOS — Point of Undetectable Outcome Superiority

**PUOS = ~10–11 fractional sets per muscle group per session**

Beyond this threshold, additional sets in the same session produce no measurable additional hypertrophic benefit and increase injury/recovery risk.

**Fractional Volume System:**
- **1.0** — primary (agonist) muscle being directly targeted
- **0.5** — synergist muscle receiving secondary stimulus
- **0** — stabilizer (isometric, not counted toward volume)

**Example:** Bench press = 3 sets
- Pectoralis Major: 3 × 1.0 = 3.0 fractional sets
- Triceps Brachii: 3 × 0.5 = 1.5 fractional sets
- Anterior Deltoid: 3 × 0.5 = 1.5 fractional sets

**max_sets_per_group (per session):** 10–11 fractional sets → effective cap ≈ **10 sets** per muscle group per session

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH** — PUOS concept is Israetel's synthesis; ~11 sets threshold referenced in his courses. Weekly volume landmarks are evidence-informed but individual variation is high (±30%).

## Section 4: SMH — Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy

### Primary Sources

1. Oranchuk, D.J. et al. (2019). "Isometric training and long-muscle-length eccentric training augment muscle thickness." *Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport*, 22(4), 449–452.
2. Werkhausen, A. et al. (2021). "Lengthened state contractions." Various PMC publications.
3. Narrative review: "Physiology of Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy and Strength Increases" — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10587333/)
4. Israetel — RP Strength: ["The Most Important Technique For Muscle Growth"](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/videos/the-most-important-technique-for-muscle-growth)
5. Counter-evidence: Nuckols et al. — [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/)

### Summary of Evidence

**What is established (HIGH confidence):**
- Training muscles in the lengthened/stretched position produces **equal or greater hypertrophy** vs. full ROM, in 5 published studies comparing lengthened partials vs. full ROM
- Exercises with peak tension at longest muscle length (e.g., cable flyes, Romanian deadlifts, incline curls) provide a stronger stretch stimulus than exercises with peak tension at short length (e.g., leg extension top position)

**What is contested (MEDIUM confidence):**
- Whether the mechanism is true "sarcomere addition / stretch-mediated hypertrophy" or simply greater mechanical tension at long lengths — debate ongoing (Nuckols, Stronger by Science)
- The specific magnitude of the advantage: studies range from "equal" to "30% greater"

### smh_volume_multiplier

**Value: 1.2–1.3** (meaning SMH exercises may produce up to 20–30% more hypertrophy per set vs. non-SMH exercises)

**Source:** Israetel, M. — RP Strength: ["The Most Important Technique For Muscle Growth"](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/videos/the-most-important-technique-for-muscle-growth) (2023). Heuristic range 1.2–1.3.

#### ⚠️ Evidence Classification: Peer-Reviewed vs Heuristic

| Claim | Evidence Type | Source |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| Lengthened-position training ≥ full ROM for hypertrophy | **PEER-REVIEWED** | 5 published RCTs (Oranchuk 2019; Werkhausen 2021; PMC10587333 systematic review) |
| Peak tension at long muscle length is mechanistically important | **PEER-REVIEWED** | Multiple EMG + mechanistic studies |
| Specific multiplier value of 1.2–1.3 | **EVIDENCE-INFORMED HEURISTIC** | Israetel's coaching estimate — no published RCT derives this exact coefficient |
| Direction of effect (SMH > non-SMH) | **HIGH CONFIDENCE** | Consistent across available studies |

**Implementation guidance:** Use **1.2** (conservative lower bound of Israetel's stated range) with a code comment documenting it as an evidence-informed heuristic, not a peer-reviewed coefficient. Example:
```python
smh_volume_multiplier: float = 1.2  # Evidence-informed heuristic (Israetel, RP Strength).
                                      # Direction of effect peer-reviewed; exact value is a
                                      # practical coaching estimate, not derived from a single RCT.
```

**Confidence: MEDIUM** — Direction of effect: HIGH confidence. Specific multiplier value: MEDIUM confidence (Israetel heuristic, not peer-reviewed coefficient).

## Section 5: Recovery Coefficients

### Primary Sources

1. Kiviniemi, A.M. et al. (2007). "Endurance training guided individually by daily HRV measurements." *European Journal of Applied Physiology*, 101, 743–751.
2. Plews, D.J. et al. (2013). "Training Adaptation and Heart Rate Variability in Elite Endurance Athletes." *Sports Medicine*, 43, 773–781.
3. Buchheit, M. (2014). "Monitoring training status with HR measures." *Frontiers in Physiology*, 5, 112.
4. PMC narrative review: "Heart Rate Variability Applications in Strength and Conditioning" — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11204851/)
5. Multi-sensor review (MDPI Sensors, 2026): — [link](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/1/3)

### HRV-Based Readiness Framework

**Baseline:** 7-day rolling average of morning resting HRV (lnRMSSD)

| HRV Status vs Baseline | Training Recommendation |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| Within normal range (±1 SD) | Proceed with planned session |
| Slightly below baseline (−1 to −2 SD, 1–2 days) | Reduce intensity by 10–15% |
| Below baseline (>−2 SD, or 3+ consecutive days down) | Reduce volume by 40% or low-intensity only |
| Significantly below (>−2 SD sustained) | Rest or active recovery only |

_Source: Kiviniemi et al. (2007); Plews et al. (2013); reproduced in PCM11204851_

### Sleep Readiness Coefficient

| Sleep Quality | Volume/Intensity Adjustment |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| Excellent (≥90% sleep score) | +0 to +5% volume (optional) |
| Good (70–89%) | Baseline (×1.0) |
| Fair (50–69%) | −10 to −15% volume |
| Poor (<50%) | −20 to −30% volume; reduce intensity |

_Source: Multi-factor readiness research; sleep score methodology from sleep science organizations (cited in Garmin/Kubios readiness frameworks, derived from Rechtschaffen & Kales sleep staging standards)_

### Stress Coefficient

| Stress Level | Adjustment |
|-------------|-----------|
| Low | ×1.0 |
| Moderate | −10% volume |
| High | −15 to −20% volume |
| Very High | −25% volume or rest |

_Source: Well-being score framework; Buchheit (2014); PMC11204851_

### Composite Readiness Score Formula (for implementation)

```
readiness_score = (hrv_component × 0.5) + (sleep_component × 0.3) + (stress_component × 0.2)
```

Where each component is normalized 0.0–1.0 (1.0 = optimal).

**Volume multiplier:** `planned_volume × readiness_score`

#### ⚠️ Evidence Classification: Peer-Reviewed vs Heuristic

| Component | Evidence Type | Source |
|-----------|--------------|--------|
| HRV as readiness/recovery marker | **PEER-REVIEWED** | Kiviniemi 2007; Plews 2013; Buchheit 2014; PMC11204851 |
| Sleep quality affects training performance | **PEER-REVIEWED** | Sleep science consensus (Rechtschaffen & Kales; AASM guidelines) |
| Stress impairs recovery and adaptation | **PEER-REVIEWED** | General physiology consensus; Buchheit 2014 |
| HRV weighting (0.5) vs Sleep (0.3) vs Stress (0.2) | **EVIDENCE-INFORMED HEURISTIC** | Proportions reflect relative objectivity of each metric; not derived from a single study |
| Composite formula structure | **EVIDENCE-INFORMED HEURISTIC** | Practical implementation design; formula not published in peer-reviewed literature |
| Specific volume multiplier thresholds (0.6, 0.75, 0.8) | **EVIDENCE-INFORMED HEURISTIC** | Derived from direction of peer-reviewed effects, not exact peer-reviewed coefficients |

**Implementation guidance:** HRV component weights (0.5/0.3/0.2) should be annotated in code as configurable evidence-informed heuristics pending athlete-specific calibration. Example:
```python
# Composite weights: evidence-informed heuristics (not peer-reviewed coefficients).
# HRV weight highest due to strongest evidence base (Kiviniemi 2007; Plews 2013).
hrv_weight: float = 0.5    # Most objective metric
sleep_weight: float = 0.3  # Moderate objectivity (self-report bias possible)
stress_weight: float = 0.2 # Most subjective; lowest weight
```

**Confidence: MEDIUM** — Framework direction: HIGH confidence. Specific numerical weights and multiplier thresholds: MEDIUM confidence (evidence-informed heuristics, not peer-reviewed coefficients from a single study).

## Section 6: Training Methodologies — Rep Ranges & Frequency

### Primary Source

**Schoenfeld, B.J. & Grgic, J. (2021).** "Loading Recommendations for Muscle Strength, Hypertrophy, and Local Endurance: A Re-Examination of the Repetition Continuum." *Sports*, 9(2), 32. DOI: 10.3390/sports9020032 — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)

### Rep Ranges by Methodology

| Methodology | Primary Rep Range | % 1RM | Frequency/Week | Key Characteristic |
|------------|-------------------|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Strength** | 1–5 | 80–100% | 2–4× per muscle | Near-maximal loading; 1RM gains favoured |
| **Hypertrophy** | 6–12 (up to 30) | 60–80% (any) | 2–4× per muscle | Volume paramount; wide load range works |
| **Endurance** | 15–30+ | <60% | 3–5× per muscle | High rep, lower load; metabolic stress |

**Key finding:** "Compelling body of literature indicates that similar whole muscle growth can be achieved across a wide spectrum of loading zones" — hypertrophy is NOT exclusive to 6–12 rep range when training to near-failure.

### Frequency Recommendations

_Source: Schoenfeld meta-analysis; Israetel RP recommendations_

| Training Goal | Frequency per Muscle Group |
|---------------|--------------------------|
| Hypertrophy | 2–4× per week (2× minimum for most) |
| Strength | 2–3× per week |
| Endurance | 3–5× per week |

**Practical implementation:** Higher frequency (3–4×/week) at lower per-session volume is equivalent to lower frequency (2×/week) at higher per-session volume, when total weekly volume is equated.

**Confidence: HIGH** — Schoenfeld 2021 is a rigorous re-examination of the repetition continuum with clear conclusions.

## Section 7: min_rest_days — Minimum Rest Between Same Muscle Group

### Primary Sources

1. **PMC6015912:** Monteiro, A.G. et al. (2018). "Effects of Consecutive Versus Non-consecutive Days of Resistance Training on Strength, Body Composition, and Red Blood Cells." — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6015912/)
2. **PMC6719818:** De Salles, B.F. et al. (2010). "Rest interval between sets in strength training." — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19691365/)
3. **Schoenfeld, B.J., Ogborn, D., & Krieger, J.W. (2016).** "Effects of Resistance Training Frequency on Measures of Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." *Sports Medicine*, 46(11), 1689–1697. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8 — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) — Provides evidence that adequate inter-session recovery (≥48h) is required to preserve hypertrophic stimulus across training sessions.

### Key Findings

| Recovery Period | Outcome |
|----------------|---------|
| 24–36 hours | Significant strength decrease in same-muscle testing |
| 48 hours | No significant decrease in strength/performance |
| 48–72 hours | Optimal recovery for most muscle groups |
| 72+ hours | Adequate for high-volume or multi-joint sessions |

**min_rest_days value: 2 days** (= 48 hours minimum)

**Rationale:**
- 24–36h: insufficient (PMC6719818 — strength reduces significantly)
- 48h: threshold at which performance is no longer impaired
- Multi-joint compounds (deadlift, squat, bench): may benefit from 72h (3 days)
- Single-joint isolation exercises: 48h typically sufficient

**Implementation for ScienceEvidence.md:**
```yaml
planning:
  min_rest_days_per_muscle_group: 2  # 48 hours minimum; baseline for isolation
  min_rest_days_compound: 3         # 72 hours recommended for multi-joint movements
```

**Confidence: HIGH** — Direct peer-reviewed evidence; consistent across multiple studies.

> **⚠️ Biomechanical Note — Why Hamstrings = 0.5 (Deadlift) vs 1.0 (RDL):**
> In conventional deadlift, the hamstrings function primarily as hip extensors during the initial pull but are loaded in a **shortened/mid-length position** at lockout (hip near full extension, knee partially extended). Peak hamstring tension occurs at a sub-maximal muscle length, reducing their hypertrophic stimulus per set.
>
> In contrast, the Romanian Deadlift (Section 8.7) is a pure hip hinge with minimal knee flexion — the hamstrings are eccentrically loaded through **maximum hip flexion** (peak stretch) before concentrically contracting. This creates peak tension at the longest muscle length, classifying the RDL as a stretch-mediated exercise for hamstrings and warranting **1.0 fractional volume** (primary target) + potential `smh_volume_multiplier` application.
>
> _Source: Biomechanical analysis per ExRx.net classification; Israetel hamstring training recommendations (RP Strength)._

## Section 9: Deload Protocol

### Primary Sources

1. Israetel, M. (Renaissance Periodization) — Volume Landmarks series; deload framework derived from MV/MRV structure already documented in Section 3.
2. Israetel, M., Case, J., & Hoffmann, J. (2019). *Scientific Principles of Strength Training.* Renaissance Periodization.
3. Zourdos, M.C. et al. — Fatigue management in periodization; referenced in PMC11204851.

### When to Deload — Triggers

| Trigger | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Volume trigger** | Weekly volume reaches or exceeds MRV for ≥1 muscle group for 2+ consecutive weeks |
| **Performance trigger** | Strength/rep performance declines on ≥2 consecutive sessions despite adequate readiness score |
| **Scheduled trigger** | Every 4–8 weeks by default (most intermediate lifters: every 4–6 weeks; advanced: every 6–8 weeks) |
| **Readiness trigger** | readiness_score < 0.65 for 3+ consecutive days |

**Implementation priority:** Performance trigger > Readiness trigger > Volume trigger > Scheduled trigger.

### Deload Protocol Structure

**Type A — Volume Deload (recommended default):**
- Reduce weekly sets to **MV level** for each muscle group
- Keep load/intensity unchanged (same weights, same RPE targets)
- Duration: 1 week
- Best for: accumulated volume fatigue without CNS burnout

**Type B — Intensity Deload:**
- Reduce load by **10–20%** across all exercises
- Keep set/rep counts unchanged
- Duration: 1 week
- Best for: joint/tendon stress, high-intensity blocks (strength phases)

**Type C — Complete Rest:**
- 5–7 days no structured training
- Indicated when: readiness_score < 0.5 sustained, illness, or accumulated stress (lifestyle + training)

### Deload Volume Targets

| Muscle Group | MV (sets/week during deload) |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| Back | 8 |
| Chest | 8 |
| Quads | 6 |
| Hamstrings | 4 |
| Glutes | 0–2 |
| Shoulders | 6 |
| Biceps | 5 |
| Triceps | 4 |
| Calves | 6 |

_Source: MV values from Israetel (RP Strength volume landmarks series), Section 3 of this document._

DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049 — [PubMed PMID 26049792](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/)

### RIR–RPE Equivalence Table

_Source: Helms et al. (2016); Zourdos et al. (2016)_

| RPE | RIR | Description |
|-----|-----|-------------|
| 10 | 0 | Maximum effort — no reps left |
| 9.5 | 0–1 | Could maybe do 1 more rep |
| 9 | 1 | Could definitely do 1 more rep |
| 8.5 | 1–2 | Could do 1, maybe 2 more reps |
| 8 | 2 | Could do 2 more reps |
| 7.5 | 2–3 | Could do 2, maybe 3 more reps |
| 7 | 3 | Could do 3 more reps |
| 6 | 4+ | Easy — 4+ reps left |
| ≤5 | 5+ | Very easy / warm-up territory |

### Recommended RPE Targets by Goal

_Source: Helms et al. (2016); Israetel RP recommendations_

| Goal | Target RPE | RIR | Rationale |
|------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| Hypertrophy | 7–8 | 2–3 | Near-failure without excessive fatigue accumulation |
| Strength | 8–9 | 1–2 | High mechanical tension; close to maximal |
| Deload / technique | 5–6 | 4–5 | Low fatigue; skill practice |
| AMRAP sets (APRE Set 3/4) | 10 | 0 | True maximum effort |

### RIR in Context of APRE and DP

- **APRE:** Set 3 and Set 4 are performed to true failure (RPE 10 / 0 RIR). Adjustment tables (Section 1) are calibrated for this.
- **Double Progression:** Working sets should be performed at RPE 7–8 (2–3 RIR) to allow consistent rep performance across all sets. Final set can be taken closer to RPE 9.
- **Volume accumulation:** Early mesocycle sets at RPE 7; late mesocycle (approaching MRV) sets drift to RPE 8–9 naturally.

## Section 11: Beginner Linear Progression

### Primary Sources

1. **Rippetoe, M. & Kilgore, L. (2011).** *Starting Strength: Basic Barbell Training* (3rd ed.). The Aasgaard Company. — Foundational LP protocol.
2. **Barbell Medicine (Baraki, A. & Feigenbaum, J.)** — Evidence-based LP framework; [Barbell Medicine LP article](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/the-beginner-prescription/). Recognized S&C practitioners.
3. **Schoenfeld, B.J. (2010).** "The Mechanisms of Muscle Hypertrophy and Their Application to Resistance Training." *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 24(10), 2857–2872. — Novice adaptation rate context.

### Defining Training Age / Experience Level

| Level | Training Age | Criteria |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Beginner** | 0–6 months consistent training | Can add weight every session; no stall in 1–2 sessions |
| **Intermediate** | 6–24 months | Progress on weekly basis; session-to-session stalls occur |
| **Advanced** | 24+ months | Progress on monthly basis; requires periodization blocks |

_Source: Rippetoe & Kilgore (2011); Barbell Medicine LP prescription_

**Practical heuristic for implementation:** If `sessions_count_for_exercise < 20` AND no stall detected → treat as beginner for that exercise.

### Beginner LP Protocol

**Core rule:** Add weight every session as long as performance criterion is met.

| Exercise Type | Weight Increment per Session |
|--------------|------------------------------|
| Squat, Deadlift | +5 kg |
| Bench Press, OHP | +2.5 kg |
| Row variations | +2.5–5 kg |
| Isolation exercises | +1.25–2.5 kg |

**Rep/Set structure for LP:** 3 sets × 5 reps (Rippetoe standard) or 3 × 8 (hypertrophy-oriented beginners). Weight incremented when all prescribed sets × reps completed at RPE ≤ 8.

### LP Stall Detection → Transition to Intermediate Protocol

| Signal | Definition | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Stall** | Failed to complete all reps for 2 consecutive sessions at same weight | Reduce weight 10%, retry |
| **Persistent stall** | 3 sessions with same weight, still failing | Transition to APRE or DP for that exercise |
| **Training age criterion** | sessions_count_for_exercise ≥ 20 AND stall detected | Transition to intermediate protocol |

### Protocol Selection Logic (for ScienceEvidence.md)

```yaml
progression:
  beginner_threshold_sessions: 20        # sessions per exercise before considering intermediate
  lp_increment_compound_lower_kg: 5.0    # squat, deadlift
  lp_increment_compound_upper_kg: 2.5    # bench, OHP, row
  lp_increment_isolation_kg: 1.25
  lp_stall_sessions: 2                   # sessions at same weight = stall
  lp_stall_weight_reset_percent: 0.10    # reduce by 10% on stall
  lp_to_intermediate_stalls: 3          # persistent stall → switch protocol
```

**Confidence: HIGH** — LP protocol is foundational resistance training consensus; increment values from Rippetoe (2011), validated by Barbell Medicine. Stall/transition criteria are evidence-informed heuristics consistent with coaching practice.
